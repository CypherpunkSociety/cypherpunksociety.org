1. Mission

Summary: Open-source digital self-defense, privacy tools, and freedom bandwidth.

Core Manifesto: Privacy and cognitive autonomy require active defense and shared infrastructure—combining EFF's Surveillance Self-Defense curriculum, Chaos Computer Club (CCC) hacker pedagogy, threat intelligence from The Citizen Lab and the Stanford Cognitive Security Task Force, and investigative methodologies from Bellingcat and OCCRP into daily practice and public-interest bandwidth at Frontier Tower.

2. Lineage: San Francisco (1992) & La Paillasse (Paris)

The Cypherpunk movement began in the San Francisco Bay Area in September 1992 when Eric Hughes, Timothy C. May, and John Gilmore convened cryptographers and civil libertarians in Oakland and San Francisco, launching the cypherpunks@toad.com mailing list on Gilmore's Sun workstation in Haight-Ashbury and coining the name cypherpunk via Berkeley writer and Mondo 2000 editor Jude Milhon ("St. Jude").

Community Era & Base Connection to Cypherpunk Society
Community Memory 1973–1992 (Berkeley, CA) World's first public BBS terminal co-created by Lee Felsenstein, Efrem Lipkin, Ken Colstad, and Jude Milhon; pioneered open-access public terminals.
The WELL 1985–Present (Sausalito & SF) Digital town square where John Perry Barlow, John Gilmore, and Mitch Kapor organized the founding of EFF in 1990; inspired toad.com as a pseudonymous counterpart.
soda.berkeley.edu Early 1990s (UC Berkeley) Home of Eric Hughes's A Cypherpunk's Manifesto (1993) and the first Type I anonymous remailers—architectural ancestors of Tor.
La Paillasse & La Quadrature du Net (π) 2010s–Present (Paris, France) Interdisciplinary biohackerspace at 226 rue Saint-Denis where biohackers and digital rights activists from La Quadrature du Net (makers of the Piphone) and Nos Oignons ran Autodéfense numérique PGP workshops—a direct spiritual precursor to Frontier Tower.

3. Curriculum & Technical Stack

Browse and contribute to our full living resource directory at github.com/CypherpunkSociety/awesome-cypherpunk:

  • Everyday Digital Hygiene & EFF Surveillance Self-Defense (SSD): Andrej Karpathy's Digital Hygiene checklist (password managers, YubiKey FIDO2/U2F, NextDNS/Pi-hole, Signal, Brave, Privacy.com, Mullvad VPN, LuLu/Little Snitch outbound firewalls) paired with EFF SSD threat modeling, border-crossing hygiene, and Privacy Guides.
  • Self-Hosting & Sovereign Infrastructure: Replacing commercial cloud services with Vaultwarden / KeePassXC, Headscale / WireGuard, Syncthing, CryptPad, Nextcloud, Immich, and local LLMs (llama.cpp / Ollama).
  • Freedom Operating Systems: Tails OS amnesic live USBs, Qubes OS compartmentalization, and GrapheneOS mobile hardening.
  • Counter-Surveillance, Censorship Measurement & Cognitive Security: Citizen Lab & Amnesty Tech commercial spyware forensics (Mobile Verification Toolkit — MVT, citizenlab/malware-indicators, citizenlab/test-lists), OONI Probe & Explorer + Censored Planet network censorship measurement, EFF Rayhunter (IMSI-catcher detection), EFF Atlas of Surveillance, DeFlock (ALPR mapping), and Stanford Cognitive Security Task Force / Stanford Trust & Safety Open Curriculum (tsbook.org) / stanfordio/skyfall.
  • OSINT, FOIA & Public-Interest Journalism: Bellingcat Online Investigation Toolkit, Bellingcat Open Source Challenge (100+ interactive OSINT puzzles), Bellingcat Research Notebooks, open-source tools at github.com/bellingcat, the UC Berkeley Human Rights Center & UN Berkeley Protocol, Micah Lee's Hacks, Leaks, and Revelations & DDoSecrets, MuckRock / DocumentCloud, OCCRP Aleph, and ProPublica Webinars & Nonprofit Explorer.
  • Aaron Swartz Open-Source Legacy Projects: Active contribution tracks for SecureDrop & SecureDrop Workstation (originally co-created by Aaron as DeadDrop, maintained by Freedom of the Press Foundation), Open Library (internetarchive/openlibrary), html2text, web.py, and Tor2web.
  • Decentralized & Secure Communications: Matrix / Element, Signal, Jitsi Meet, OnionShare, Briar, Bluesky (AT Protocol), Mastodon (ActivityPub), Nostr, and Telegram free-speech channels.

5. Bandwidth Advocacy Playbook

  1. Building-Wide Tor Snowflake Proxies: Zero-friction WebRTC censorship circumvention via the Snowflake browser extension or standalone container—safe for shared coworking IPs.
  2. Dedicated obfs4 Bridges & Middle Relays: Isolated VLAN and "100 Mbps for Freedom" resident VPS pledge running non-exit relays under Tor Relay Operator guidelines.
  3. Non-Profit Exit Node Peering: Sponsoring and hosting peering workshops with Emerald Onion (Fremont Cabal IX) and Nos Oignons.
  4. Permanent Tails Flashing Terminal: Dedicated Linux verification station at Frontier Tower with bulk USB-C media.
