1. Harden your laptop

Three attackers, three controls. A lost or stolen laptop is beaten by full-disk encryption and a strong login secret. An evil maid (brief physical access, device returned) is beaten only by verified or measured boot and firmware write-protection, which most checklists skip. Remote compromise (phishing, a malicious document, a browser exploit) is beaten by patching, sandboxing, an outbound firewall and Lockdown Mode. Decide which of the three you are actually defending against before you touch a setting.

Do tonight

  1. Start from EFF's Your Security Plan and pick your attacker.
  2. macOS 27 Golden Gate (released Sept 14 2026; 27.2 on Oct 26; Apple-silicon only), in order: FileVault on.
  3. Lockdown Mode on if you are a plausible target. It blocks most Messages attachments, web JIT, FaceTime from strangers, wired accessories while locked, 2G/3G and config profiles; iOS/macOS 27 added per-contact exclusions. Apple told TechCrunch in March 2026 it knows of no successful attack on a device running it.
  4. Advanced Data Protection for iCloud, with the recovery key written down. Mail, Contacts and Calendar stay unencrypted even then. UK users lost ADP in Feb 2025 under a Technical Capability Notice and the Tribunal appeal is still running.
  5. Gatekeeper on "App Store and identified developers"; SIP left on (csrutil status); Full Security in Startup Security Utility. There is no firmware password on Apple silicon: Apple's Platform Security Guide (Aug 2026 edition) says FileVault plus Activation Lock replaces it.
  6. Add an outbound firewall: Little Snitch ($59, version 7 in beta) or the free LuLu 4.5.7. For allow-listing what may execute, Santa (now North Pole Security, Apache-2.0, v2026.9 validated on macOS 27) is the tool Google built for its own fleet.
  7. Audit the new Full Disk Access grants that macOS 27 prompts for when AI agents ask.
  8. Linux. Privacy Guides (July 2026) recommends Fedora, openSUSE Tumbleweed or Arch for a traditional desktop, Fedora Atomic or NixOS for an immutable one, Whonix or Tails for anonymity, and Qubes OS, secureblue or Kicksecure for security. Turn on LUKS at install time, not later; prefer Wayland; keep SELinux or AppArmor; use Flatpak as the sandbox and skip Firejail (setuid root, large attack surface). Verified boot is "still largely unavailable" on mainstream distros; the step up is enrolling your own Secure Boot keys with sbctl and sealing LUKS to the TPM. Qubes OS 4.3.2 (Oct 2 2026) is current; its certified-hardware list is thirteen models, newest the Star Labs StarFighter and StarBook Horizon (Oct 21 2026), with NovaCustom and NitroPad in the middle and no Framework model on it. Tails 7.16 (Oct 30 2026) now ships every two weeks in step with Tor Browser 16.
  9. Windows 11. Buy Pro, because Home lacks full BitLocker, Hyper-V and Sandbox. Since 24H2 Device Encryption is on by default on clean installs, with the recovery key escrowed to your Microsoft account; back the key up yourself (manage-bde -protectors -get C:) and on Pro add a TPM+PIN pre-boot authenticator, because the April 2026 cumulative update threw some PCs into recovery. Recall is opt-in and removable on Copilot+ PCs; Signal Desktop blocks it anyway. Turn on Core Isolation. Windows 10 ended support Oct 14 2025.
  10. Firmware. Microsoft's 2011 Secure Boot certificates expired in June and October 2026; a PC without the 2023 replacements in its DB and KEK still boots but no longer gets boot-manager or revocation updates (KB5062710). PKfail (July 2024) found AMI test keys shipped as production Platform Keys across roughly 800 product lines, and LogoFAIL put a bootkit in the boot-logo parser, so "Secure Boot on" is only as good as the OEM's key hygiene. The open alternatives are Heads (coreboot plus a TPM-measured boot with a Nitrokey or Librem Key; tamper-evident, not tamper-proof) and 3mdeb's Dasharo on NovaCustom, MSI, Protectli and, since v0.9.0 in Aug 2026, AMD AM5. Framework has upstream coreboot work (26.09 added the Framework 12) but no shipping open firmware yet.
  11. Browser, passwords, keys. Mullvad Browser, Firefox with the Privacy Guides settings, or Brave when you need Chromium; Tor Browser 16 for anonymity only. uBlock Origin is dead on Chrome since Chrome 138 (July 2025) removed the last override; Firefox and Brave keep it. Test yourself on Cover Your Tracks. Bitwarden (switch the KDF to Argon2id) or KeePassXC. YubiKey 5 NFC is $58 and ships firmware 5.8, which matters because EUCLEAK (Sept 2024) made every key below 5.7 cloneable by a lab with physical possession and there is no field upgrade. Passkeys are mainstream now (FIDO counts 5 billion and 36% of logins at large sites in Oct 2026): keep them in the password manager for convenience and on a hardware key for the accounts that matter.

2. Harden your phone

The one principle that beats forensics: after a reboot the file-system keys are not in memory (Before First Unlock); after the first unlock they are, and nearly every Cellebrite or GrayKey extraction of a consumer phone is an After First Unlock extraction. The Oct 30 2025 leak of a Cellebrite briefing slide listed every locked Pixel 9 on GrapheneOS as inaccessible and stock Pixels as partly extractable even BFU.

Do tonight

  1. Power the phone off before a border, a protest or an arrest; set GrapheneOS auto-reboot short; rely on iOS's 72-hour Inactivity Reboot (iOS 18.1) otherwise. Use a passcode, not biometrics: the Ninth Circuit in US v. Payne (April 2024) held a compelled thumbprint non-testimonial while the D.C. Circuit went the other way in 2025, so biometrics are a legal lottery.
  2. iPhone (iOS 27, Sept 14 2026). Lockdown Mode on if you could be a target; Stolen Device Protection with "Require Security Delay: Always"; Advanced Data Protection with a recovery key; Safety Check run once to see what you share with whom. The iPhone 17 and Air introduced Memory Integrity Enforcement (always-on tagged memory across the kernel and 70+ system processes), the single biggest reason a targeted person should carry a 17-or-newer.
  3. Apple's threat notifications have now gone to users in 150+ countries; the Aug 13 2026 wave hit 110 countries and the late-October wave 99, including Bundestag members per Germany's BSI. If you get one, Apple's own advice is Lockdown Mode plus the Access Now helpline. iVerify Basic ($0.99, one scan a month) is the consumer check; Amnesty's MVT (2026.5.12) is the real one and needs a backup or sysdiagnose and a volunteer who has run it before.
  4. Android: GrapheneOS or nothing. It is the only Android OS Privacy Guides recommends and the only one that survived Google's June 2025 decision to publish Android 16 to AOSP without Pixel device trees. Supported today: Pixel 6 through 10, latest release 2026110200 on an Android 17 base; buy a Pixel 8 or later for hardware MTE and seven years of patches. The March 2026 Motorola partnership means a non-Pixel option no earlier than 2027.
  5. On GrapheneOS turn on: auto-reboot (default 18 h), a duress PIN (irreversible wipe including eSIMs), charging-only USB when locked, LTE-only, Storage and Contact Scopes, and run the Auditor app for attestation. CalyxOS paused in Aug 2025, returned July 1 2026 and began an Android 17 rollout Oct 1; LineageOS and /e/OS lack verified boot on most devices. On a stock Android 16 phone, Google's one-toggle Advanced Protection (USB data block, sideload block, 2G block, MTE, intrusion logging) is the floor.
  6. SIM swap, carrier side. Since the FCC's order took effect in July 2024 every carrier must offer a free lock: AT&T Wireless Account Lock, Verizon Number Lock plus SIM Protection, T-Mobile Port Out Protection plus SIM Protection. Turn them on tonight, set a carrier PIN, and move every 2FA off SMS.
  7. Messaging. Signal: set a username and "Who can see my number: Nobody". It added opt-in secure backups (Sept 2025), the SPQR post-quantum ratchet (Oct 2025) and automatic key verification (Aug 2026), and its latest subpoena response (D.C. grand jury, Nov 3 2026) still produced only a registration date and a last-connection date. WhatsApp encrypts content with the same protocol but Meta keeps the contact graph, group membership, timestamps and IP. For shutdowns: Meshtastic (LoRa; change the public default channel key; no forward secrecy), Briar (Tor/Bluetooth; in maintenance mode since July 9 2026), and Bitchat (Bluetooth mesh, no audit yet). They are availability tools, not confidentiality tools.
  8. Stalkerware. Citizen Lab confirmed Paragon's Graphite on European journalists' iPhones in June 2025 and Italy ended its contract; Intellexa's Predator rebuilt its infrastructure after US sanctions, and Treasury quietly delisted three Intellexa-linked people on Jan 2 2026. The far more common threat is stalkerware installed by someone with your passcode: run iPhone Safety Check, check the Android device-admin and accessibility lists, and use the Coalition Against Stalkerware resources, whose Sept 2026 test with EFF found antivirus detection still inconsistent.

3. Harden your Wi-Fi router

The stable OpenWrt series is 25.12 (March 5 2026, Linux 6.12, over 2,200 devices). Two things changed for anyone who last flashed a router in 2024: opkg is gone, replaced by Alpine's apk (cheatsheet), and attended sysupgrade is installed by default, with the owut command-line updater on larger-flash devices. The current service release is 25.12.6 (Oct 18 2026), which fixed five unauthenticated uhttpd bugs, a CVSS 9.9 ser2net root-command bug and an rpcd symlink bug that let delegated users read /etc/shadow. Treat 24.10 as end-of-life. No 26.x branch has been announced.

Do tonight

The ten-minute checklist, with the real option names (wiki paths from the security overview):

  1. Root password at first login; then bind admin to LAN only: dropbear.@dropbear[0].Interface='lan', uhttpd listening on the LAN address, WAN zone input REJECT (secure access).
  2. Wi-Fi encryption 'sae' (WPA3) or 'sae-mixed', with ieee80211w '2' for required management-frame protection (wifi basics); 25.12.1 has a known 802.11r-plus-WPA3 issue on some clients.
  3. A guest SSID with isolate '1' and an IoT VLAN, each its own interface and firewall zone forwarding to WAN only (guest WLAN, DSA VLANs).
  4. Encrypted upstream DNS with https-dns-proxy (DoH), stubby (DoT) or a recursive unbound; then DNS hijack: redirect port 53 and drop outbound 853 so TVs and speakers cannot bypass the resolver.
  5. adblock or the lighter adblock-lean at the resolver.
  6. A WireGuard server so you can reach home from a café instead of trusting its network.
  7. Never install miniupnpd; leave WPS off (OpenWrt ships neither).
  8. Updates through attended sysupgrade or owut; remote syslog via log_ip in /etc/config/system.
  9. The ISP's box. Put the Xfinity gateway in bridge mode (10.0.0.1, Gateway, At a Glance), which also switches off "CyberSecure", the CUJO AI service that analyses your Wi-Fi traffic under a company promise not to look at encrypted content; TR-069 remote management of the gateway firmware stays.
  10. If you own an ASUS: GreyNoise's AyySSHush (May 2025) planted a persistent SSH key on 9,000 ASUS routers via CVE-2023-39780 and survives firmware updates; check authorized_keys and factory reset.

The state's box. Salt Typhoon (joint advisory Aug 27 2025) got into at least nine US carriers through known edge-device bugs and CISA cannot confirm eviction, which is why the only honest assumption is that carrier-side metadata and lawful-intercept systems are readable and everything that matters goes end-to-end encrypted. On March 23 2026 the FCC put every foreign-made consumer router on its Covered List: ASUS, Netgear and eero have conditional approval, TP-Link does not, and already-sold routers may receive firmware updates only through March 1 2027 with the rest undecided. Owning a TP-Link is legal; patching it may get harder. The FBI's May 7 2025 flash named thirteen end-of-life Linksys and Cisco models being sold as residential proxies. Flashing OpenWrt onto a supported old router is the fix for exactly that.

Hardware

The OpenWrt One is the clinic unit. The OpenWrt One, built with Banana Pi on a MediaTek MT7981B (Wi-Fi 6, 2.5 GbE with PoE-in and 1 GbE, NVMe slot, a NOR recovery flash that makes it very hard to brick), launched Dec 2024 at $89.99 with case, antennas and power supply, $10 of which goes to Software Freedom Conservancy; in Nov 2026 it lists at $89 to $111 on Banana Pi's AliExpress store and $125 as a kit at Ameridroid. The OpenWrt Two, proposed Feb 2025 with GL.iNet on the MediaTek MT7988 (Wi-Fi 7, 10G SFP+, four 2.5G ports, "$250 region", "late 2025"), has not shipped: as of April 2026 the maintainers say only that there will be an announcement when there is something to announce, and the forum guesses at 2027 and about $300.

Above it: CZ.NIC's Turris Omnia publishes board schematics per revision, and the new Omnia NG Wired (IPQ9574, 10G SFP+, no Wi-Fi) was shown in March 2026 at about €350; the Banana Pi BPI-R4 (Filogic 880, roughly $100 plus a $74 Wi-Fi 7 card) is the tinkerer's board. A warning on GL.iNet: the Flint 2 (MT6000) runs upstream OpenWrt, but the Flint 3 (Qualcomm, $230) ships a fork of 23.05 on a 5.4 kernel and is "not yet supported" upstream, and GL.iNet's April 2025 advisory covered four CVEs across twenty models. For a wired firewall in front of a dumb access point, Privacy Guides recommends only OpenWrt and OPNsense; OPNsense 26.7 (July 2026) and pfSense CE 2.9 (Aug 2026) both run on a Protectli (coreboot option, about $340) or a ZimaBoard 2 ($339). Ubiquiti is closed.

OHSNAP, the tier above. Noisebridge's OHSNAP (Open Hardware for Secure Networks and Privacy) aimed one level higher: an open-source platform for secure networks with an auditable hardware root of trust and supplier-diversified components, starting with the OHSNAP Firewall, a single-board computer running open firmware and an open OS as the edge node of a home network. It ran as an all-volunteer weekly Thursday workgroup at Noisebridge through 2022 and 2023. We found no repository, board design or shipped hardware after that, so treat it as dormant until someone at Noisebridge confirms. Ask at 2169 Mission whether the workgroup still meets.

Clinic: flash your router

Month 4, or first if hardware is on the table. 90 minutes, same shape as the Tails USB station: a verified image, one irreversible step, then a checklist. Participants leave with OpenWrt running on a supported router and the ten-minute hardening done.

  • Hardware decides the clinic. The inventory of old Netgear and Google WiFi units needs a model-by-model check against the OpenWrt table of hardware; original Google WiFi pucks (AC-1304) and Netgear R7800, R6220, R6350, WAX202 and WAX206 are supported, Broadcom-based Nighthawks like the R7000 are not. Anything with 8 MB of flash or less is deprecated.
  • One supported unit on the table that everyone flashes together; participants' own routers as a side track after a hardware-list check.
  • The checklist is the content: root password, WPA3, no WAN-side admin, a guest or IoT VLAN, DNS over TLS, attended sysupgrade for updates. People photograph this slide and take it home.
  • It stacks: the flashed router becomes the host for the Snowflake proxy, the DNS resolver from the Pi-hole clinic, and the Kiwix mirror.
  • Why it is more secure, said honestly: OpenWrt is maintained and minimal where vendor firmware is abandoned and bloated. The WiFi radio driver is the same closed blob either way, and a misconfigured OpenWrt is worse than stock.

4. Harden against network surveillance

With plain DNS your ISP sees every hostname; with DNS over HTTPS or TLS only the resolver sees that, but the TLS handshake still announces the hostname in the clear unless Encrypted Client Hello is on. None of this stops the ISP selling the rest: Congress repealed the FCC's broadband-privacy rules in April 2017, the FTC's 2021 report on six ISPs documented what Comcast, AT&T and Verizon collect, and only Maine's 2020 opt-in law and Minnesota's 2025 privacy act constrain it.

Do tonight

  1. Turn on encrypted DNS and check Encrypted Client Hello at Cloudflare's ESNI test. ECH became RFC 9849 in March 2026; Firefox and Chrome send it when DoH is active, Safari does not, and a 2026 measurement found 99.99% of deployments are Cloudflare (on by default for free zones) with Meta the only other large one.
  2. Pick a resolver: Quad9 (Zurich; won the Sony case on appeal in Dec 2023), Cloudflare 1.1.1.1 (truncated IPs, logs gone in 25 hours, audited), NextDNS for per-device policy, or your own Unbound. Mullvad shut its public DNS on Nov 2 2026 and now funds Quad9, so delete that profile.
  3. VPN, chosen honestly. A VPN moves trust from the ISP to the VPN; it is not anonymity, and both Privacy Guides and CISA say so. Its criteria (WireGuard, multi-hop, kill switch, cash or Monero, audits, RAM-only servers, public leadership) leave Mullvad, Proton VPN and IVPN. Mullvad is €5 flat, takes cash in an envelope, had nothing to hand over when Swedish police raided it in April 2023, and ships DAITA traffic-shaping against fingerprinting; the July 2026 donation controversy over a co-founder's political gift led the Tor Project to pause co-branding on Oct 9 2026 while keeping the engineering relationship, which you should know before recommending it in the room. The US VPN-ban bills failed for now: Wisconsin's was stripped and vetoed (veto sustained May 13 2026), Michigan's HB 4938 has not moved. For a VPN you control: Algo, Headscale or NetBird on a VPS plus the WireGuard server on your router.
  4. Tor. Tor Browser 16.0 (Oct 29 2026) and the Rust implementation Arti 2.8 are current; bridges on offer are obfs4, WebTunnel and Snowflake. At home, run a bridge (low risk, one megabit) and never an exit.
  5. The café. On someone else's Wi-Fi the threats are the evil twin, the captive portal and the probe request: use WPA3 networks where you can, keep MAC randomisation on (iOS 18 added a "rotating" mode that changes every two weeks and defaults on for open networks; Android randomises per-SSID but only re-randomises on open networks if the OEM allowed it), and tunnel out.
  6. Cellular. 5G hides the IMSI with SUCI but a catcher simply downgrades you to 4G or 2G, so turn 2G off (Lockdown Mode and GrapheneOS both do) and build EFF's Rayhunter (v0.13.0, Sept 2026, on a $20 Orbic hotspot) to see when it happens. CISA's Dec 18 2024 guidance after Salt Typhoon says it plainly: Signal, FIDO keys, no SMS codes, a carrier PIN.
  7. Metadata. Signal's subpoena record still shows registration and last-connection dates only; Apple holds iMessage capability-query logs for 25 days and your iCloud backup keys unless Advanced Data Protection is on; WhatsApp can produce near-real-time contact and timing data under a pen register; iOS 26.5 (May 2026) finally shipped end-to-end encrypted RCS with Google Messages, metadata still collected. Email encrypts bodies, not headers: alias it.
  8. Measure yourself with OONI Probe, dnsleaktest, Cover Your Tracks, and tcpdump on your own OpenWrt box.

The legal layer moved this year. Section 702 of FISA expired at midnight on June 12 2026 after the House rejected reauthorisation 198 to 218, though collection continues under FISC certifications approved in March that run to March 2027, the opinion is still classified past its deadline, and a December NDAA rider is the expected next attempt. The Fourth Amendment Is Not For Sale Act died in the Senate in Jan 2025 and has not been reintroduced, so ICE's purchases go on: Palantir's $30M ImmigrationOS, a $125M Thomson Reuters deal, LexisNexis feeding Palantir, phone-location data on a leaked tool list, a facial-recognition app for a thousand agencies. Flock's cameras are the local front: Evanston found ICE searches of its data despite Illinois law, Oakland rejected a $2M contract in Nov 2025 and stalled a $2.25M one in Sept 2026 pending audits, SF faces a class action over 1.6 million out-of-state and federal queries, Flock's CEO conceded indirect ICE access in July 2026, and the Institute for Justice counts 273 jurisdictions cancelling since Jan 2025. San Francisco's own 2019 facial-recognition ban was hollowed out by Prop E (March 2024); an SFPD audit found about 300 improper plate-reader queries in May 2026 and Secure Justice is suing.

5. Cripple consumer-device monitoring

A device that cannot reach the vendor cannot report to it, so the IoT VLAN with WAN blocked (see the router workshop) is the universal fix, and local-only replacements exist for almost everything. This workshop adds the menu paths and the other devices in the house.

Do tonight

  1. TV menus (Malwarebytes' March 2026 list; labels drift by model year). Samsung: General & Privacy, Terms & Privacy, Privacy Choices, uncheck Viewing Information Services. LG: General, System, Additional Settings, Live Plus off. Vizio: System, Reset & Admin, Viewing Data off. Roku and Roku TVs: Privacy, Smart TV Experience, "Use info from TV inputs" off. Sony and Google TV: Privacy, Samba Interactive TV off. Fire TV: Privacy Settings, Collect App Usage Data off. Apple TV has no ACR; turn off analytics sharing. Texas sued Samsung, LG, Sony, Hisense and TCL on Dec 15 2025; Samsung settled Feb 26 2026 with ACR off for Texans unless they opt in, Hisense is under a restraining order, and the LG settlement claim is single-source.
  2. Cars. Run your VIN through Privacy4Cars' free Vehicle Privacy Report, which tells you whether the car has its own modem and files the opt-out and deletion requests as your agent; then turn off connected services in the maker's app. The FTC finalised its GM order on Jan 14 2026: twenty years, a five-year ban on selling location or driving data to credit bureaus, affirmative consent per feature, and a right for any US driver to get a copy and demand deletion. Pulling the telematics fuse works on some models and kills automatic crash notification and sometimes unrelated functions; treat it as a hobbyist option, not advice. California's SB 1394 (2024) lets abuse survivors cut an abuser's connected-car access.
  3. Speakers. Amazon removed the "Do Not Send Voice Recordings" option on March 28 2025 to make room for Alexa+, so every Echo now streams audio to the cloud; set Alexa Privacy to "Don't save recordings" and auto-delete, and accept that the device is a microphone you cannot audit. Google's Gemini for Home reached every Nest speaker since 2016 in Oct 2025; its 2026 speaker at least has a switch that cuts microphone power. Apple paid $95M in Jan 2025 to settle Siri eavesdropping claims. The auditable alternative is Home Assistant's Voice Preview Edition ($69, local wake word and speech-to-phrase, Whisper on an N100 if you want full dictation).
  4. Doorbells and cameras. Ring dropped warrantless police "Request for Assistance" in Jan 2024, then signed with Axon (April 2025), shipped facial recognition as Familiar Faces (Dec 2025), announced a Flock Safety integration (Oct 2025), ran the Search Party ad at Super Bowl LX on Feb 8 2026, and cancelled the Flock deal five days later under bipartisan fire. Wyze showed 13,000 users strangers' camera thumbnails in Feb 2024; Eufy's "local-only" cameras were caught uploading faces in 2022. The local stack is RTSP cameras (Reolink, Amcrest) on the IoT VLAN feeding Frigate 0.18.2 (Oct 2026) for on-box object detection, or UniFi Protect with cloud access left off, both inside Home Assistant.
  5. Wearables, glasses, DNA. Oura's consumer cloud is separate from its Department of Defense tenant but not end-to-end encrypted; Whoop is being sued for sharing heart-rate data with Segment; Fitbit accounts not migrated to Google by May 19 2026 were deleted from July 15. For period tracking use Euki or Drip, which store nothing off the phone. 6. Protect your online profile and social media

Lock the accounts that can reset the others first, then dox yourself before someone else does. The data-broker opt-outs are their own clinic ("Delete yourself", below in the calendar).

Do tonight

  1. Two hardware keys or two passkeys on every account that can reset the others (email, Apple or Google, the password manager, the carrier). Google's Advanced Protection Program and Apple's Security Keys (two to six keys; lose them all and you are locked out for good) are the strongest consumer settings that exist. Passkey portability is half-shipped: the FIDO Credential Exchange Format reached proposed-standard status in 2025 and iOS 26 added export, so you are no longer locked into one manager.
  2. Take email off the critical path: a different alias per service through SimpleLogin ($36 a year), addy.io, Apple's Hide My Email or DuckDuckGo's free masks; Firefox Relay still works at five free masks but its paid tiers are waitlisted.
  3. Dox yourself. Run NYT Open's How to Dox Yourself against EFF's Manage Your Digital Footprint module (reviewed May 2025): search your name, handles, phone, address and old usernames across Google, people-search sites and face search, then file removals. California's DROP opened Jan 1 2026, brokers have had to honour it since Aug 1, half a million Californians had signed up by Aug 25, and CalPrivacy brought its first Delete Act enforcement actions on Aug 11 and 13 and launched "Delete Week" on Oct 12 2026. PEN America's Online Harassment Field Manual is the playbook when it goes wrong; Block Party was bought by DeleteMe in March 2026, and Crash Override and Tall Poppy both look dormant.
  4. Your face. PimEyes' opt-out form wants a face photo and a redacted ID and suppresses only its own results (Cook County's advice: black out the name on the ID). Clearview's equity settlement for scraping 3 billion faces was vacated by the Seventh Circuit on July 13 2026 after 22 states objected, the UK tribunal confirmed the ICO's jurisdiction in Oct 2025, and ICE signed a $9.2M sole-source Clearview contract on Sept 5 2025. Image cloaking (Fawkes, LowKey) has not been updated since 2022 and should be assumed defeated; Glaze and Nightshade are for artists, not faces.
  5. Opt out of model training where a switch exists: Meta's objection form (EU and UK), LinkedIn's Data for Generative AI Improvement toggle, X's Grok setting (check in-app, reports conflict). Strip EXIF before posting (exiftool -all= photo.jpg, or the iOS share sheet's Location off), and know that C2PA content credentials, now in the Pixel 10 camera, can carry GPS and device serials even though the spec does not require identity.
  6. Platforms are deanonymising by default. X's "About this account" panel has shown every account's inferred country since Nov 21 2025, which outs anyone pseudonymous without a VPN; switch it to region in Privacy and safety or stop posting from a home IP. The age-verification wave is the bigger shift: UK age checks since July 25 2025, Australia's under-16 ban since Dec 10 2025, Texas's app-store law enjoined on Dec 23 2025, California's AB 1043 (operative Jan 1 2027) sending an age bracket from the OS rather than an ID upload. The Discord breach disclosed Oct 3 2025, in which a support vendor exposed about 70,000 government-ID photos, is the standing argument against ID-upload age checks.
  7. Migrate and compartmentalise: Mastodon moves followers but not posts; AT Protocol account migration with goat moves everything, including off a hostile server. A pseudonymous account is made over Tor with an alias email, never opened from the phone that holds the real one.
  8. Browser and the opt-out signal. uBlock Origin on Firefox (Chrome is done), Privacy Badger, and Global Privacy Control turned on, because California now fines companies that ignore it: Honda $632,500 (March 2025), Todd Snyder $345,178 (May), Tractor Supply $1.35M (Sept 30 2025). AB 566, signed Oct 8 2025, forces every browser sold in California to ship the signal from Jan 1 2027.

7. Your risk when travelling abroad

The US border is the one to plan for. CBP searched 55,318 devices in FY2025 and 52,080 in the first three quarters of FY2026 (47,517 basic, 4,564 forensic), a third more than the same period a year earlier, and it refuses to break out citizens from non-citizens or airports from land borders (Freedom of the Press Foundation, Nov 4 2026). Directive 3340-049B, in force since Jan 1 2026, lets officers do a basic search with no suspicion at all and a forensic copy on "reasonable suspicion" or a "national security concern"; what they copy goes into the Automated Targeting System for up to 15 years.

Do tonight

The travel-device checklist (EFF and FPF's journalist checklist, June 2025, plus the cases below):

  1. Carry the minimum, ideally a travel phone with nothing you cannot show.
  2. Log out of cloud accounts and delete the apps, since CBP policy puts cloud data out of scope only if the device cannot reach it; empty the trash.
  3. Full-disk encryption, long alphanumeric passcode, biometrics off, Lockdown Mode on (it blocks accessory connections while locked and has stopped at least one extraction), and power fully off before the line.
  4. Decide in advance whether you will unlock, and if you do, type it yourself. Never lie, never obstruct.
  5. Wipe before you fly, never during the search: in US v. Tunick an activist who typed a GrapheneOS duress password during a January 2025 secondary inspection was charged with destroying property to prevent seizure.
  6. If the device is seized, take the CBP Form 6051D receipt, write down names, badge numbers and times at once, and email borders@eff.org. A Faraday bag stops radios, not a hand search.
  7. Travel eSIMs (Airalo's policy logs IP and usage) plus a VPN beat KYC-registered local SIMs in Turkey, India, the UAE and China.
  8. If Apple ever sends you a threat notification abroad, treat the device as compromised and go to the Access Now helpline.
  9. Know what refusal costs for your status. A citizen cannot be denied entry for refusing a passcode, but the device can be held (five days by default, extendable) and you will be flagged for future secondaries; lying to CBP is a federal crime. A green-card holder cannot be refused entry for silence alone but can be held and referred onward. A visa or ESTA holder can be turned around for refusing or for an "empty phone": Alistair Kitchen (LAX, June 2025, laptop seized and passcode demanded over his Columbia-protest writing) and Rasha Alawieh (Logan, March 2025, removed over photos in a deleted folder) are the templates. Non-citizens must also now show F, M and J visa social media set to public (State Dept, June 18 2025); CBP's Dec 10 2025 notice proposing five years of social-media handles and ten years of email addresses for every ESTA applicant was not final as of January 2026. Facial comparison at every port became mandatory for all non-citizens on Dec 26 2025; citizens may still opt out and ask for a manual document check.

The courts are not coming. The Second Circuit joined six others in US v. Alisigwe (Sept 17 2026) holding that manual searches need no suspicion; the warrant ruling in US v. Smith (S.D.N.Y. 2023) is an outlier on appeal; EFF's next shot is US v. Roggio in the Third Circuit.

Elsewhere. The UK's Schedule 7 has no right to silence and refusing a password is itself an offence (Fahad Ansari, Holyhead, Aug 2025). Canada's CBSA requires the password once it decides to examine, with seizure for refusal. Hong Kong's National Security Law rules since March 23 2026 let police compel unlocking, including for transit passengers (US consulate alert March 26). Mainland China's state-security police may inspect devices on the spot under rules in force since July 1 2024; VPN use is a grey zone with no public case against a tourist. Russia restricted WhatsApp and Telegram calls in Aug 2025, made the state messenger MAX mandatory on new phones from Sept 1 2025, and logged 2,129 mobile-internet shutdowns in August 2025 alone. The UAE criminalises VPN "misuse" and unlicensed VoIP; Indian districts in Jammu and Kashmir ban VPNs by two-month order with random phone stops. The EU's Entry/Exit System took fingerprints and face at every external border from April 10 2026, with ETIAS pre-authorisation due in the fourth quarter. For the big picture, Freedom on the Net 2026 records a sixteenth straight year of decline with the US flat at 73/100, RSF ranks the US 64th of 180, and CPJ counted 330 jailed journalists on Dec 1 2025.

Access Now's #KeepItOn count for 2025 was 313 shutdowns in 52 countries, a record, with 75 still running into 2026. Afghanistan had a 48-hour nationwide blackout on Sept 29 2025. Russia's mobile-internet shutdowns are now routine. This is the demand side; the Society is supply.

Do tonight

  2. This month: an obfs4 or WebTunnel bridge on a $5 VPS or at home: no exit traffic, so no abuse mail. WebTunnel was vital in Russia until the bridges were enumerated in mid-2025, which is why Tor now hands them out through its Telegram bot and always needs more.

9. Follow the money

The Offshore Leaks (2013), Swiss Leaks (2015), Panama Papers (2016), Paradise Papers (2017), FinCEN Files (2020), Pandora Papers (2021) and Suisse Secrets (2022) are the other half of this curriculum. The tools that protect a source (SecureDrop, Signal, air-gapped analysis) are the tools that made the leaks possible, and the methods that cracked the networks (document processing, graph analysis, public registries) are open source with the data downloadable. What the leaks showed. The chokepoint is the intermediary, not the owner: Mossack Fonseca sold through about 14,000 banks, law firms and formation agents, and a few dozen providers and a few hundred banks service most of the world’s shell companies. Layering is standardised: a BVI or Panama company, nominee directors and shareholders supplied by the provider, bearer shares or a Panamanian private foundation on top, a bank account in Switzerland, Luxembourg or Cyprus, and the money parked in London, New York or Dubai property, yachts and art. Nominees are the tell: a handful of people appeared as director of thousands of companies, and shared registered addresses link entities that are otherwise unconnected on paper. Due diligence was theatre: the firm found sanctioned Syrian and North Korean fronts among its clients only after journalists asked. Secrecy migrates under pressure: once the OECD’s automatic exchange of account data began, the Pandora Papers showed trusts moving to South Dakota, Nevada and Delaware, which do not report, and the US has topped the Tax Justice Network’s Financial Secrecy Index since 2022. And most of it is legal, which is the finding: Alstadsæter, Johannesen and Zucman matched Swiss Leaks and Panama Papers names to Scandinavian tax records and found the top 0.01 percent evade about a quarter of their taxes, and Zucman’s estimate is that about 8 percent of global household financial wealth sits offshore. ICIJ coordinated roughly 370 journalists in 76 countries on the Panama Papers alone; governments have since recovered over $1.3 billion, Iceland’s prime minister resigned, Pakistan’s was disqualified, and Mossack Fonseca closed in 2018.

Do tonight

  1. Load the graph. Download the ICIJ Offshore Leaks Database (810,000+ entities from every leak, CSV and a Neo4j dump via offshoreleaks-data-packages), open it in Neo4j Desktop, and run three queries: the officers with the highest degree (the professional nominees), the addresses shared by the most entities, and every entity within two hops of a San Francisco address.
  2. Resolve a name. Take one person from the graph and check them against OpenSanctions, OpenCorporates and OCCRP Aleph. Note how often the same registered agent or address recurs.
  3. Find the property. Pick a Manhattan address and pull the deed from ACRIS; pick a London one and look up the owning company at Companies House and the Register of Overseas Entities. Ships are in Equasis, aircraft in the FAA registry.
  4. Process documents. Install Datashare on a laptop and index a public document set from DDoSecrets; it does the OCR, entity extraction and search ICIJ used, and Micah Lee’s Hacks, Leaks, and Revelations walks through the same workflow with Aleph.
  5. Learn the structure. Read Garcia-Bernardo and colleagues on conduits and sinks: the Netherlands, UK, Switzerland, Singapore and Ireland route the money, and BVI, Cayman, Jersey and Bermuda hold it. That map explains which registry to check next.
  6. Protect the source. The Panama Papers source contacted Süddeutsche Zeitung over encrypted chat and insisted on it. Know which newsrooms run SecureDrop and how to reach one over Tor before you ever need to.

Clinic: Dark web for defenders (month 2)

90 minutes. Participants leave with a Tor Browser they understand, a Snowflake proxy running, and their own breach exposure checked without handing anyone new data.

  • Threat model first, per EFF's security plan: who wants your data, what can they already buy.
  • History in 15 minutes: the Glen Park library, two corrupt agents, the pardon; the point that cryptography did not break, opsec did.
  • Hands-on: HIBP and Apple Password Monitoring as the k-anonymity model; why Google's report died; what ransomware.live shows about your employer.
  • Demo, not install: VoidAccess as an example of self-hosted threat intel, with the crawling-liability caveat from its HN thread.
  • Run a Snowflake proxy; show Tor Metrics usage from Iran during the Feb to May 2026 shutdown.

Clinic: Delete yourself (month 3)

90 minutes, bring a laptop. Participants file DROP (Californians), Permission Slip, and the top ten people-search opt-outs from the Big Ass list, and freeze credit at six bureaus.

  • Open by showing one volunteer's Radaris and Spokeo pages, then the Locate X abortion-clinic demonstration.
  • Rule: no government ID uploads to intermediaries; read retention terms aloud for one service.
  • Set a calendar reminder for the 3-month recheck before leaving.
